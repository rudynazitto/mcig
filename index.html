Why This Matters

The Massachusetts energy efficiency landscape is at a critical juncture. In February 2025, the Department of Public Utilities (DPU) enacted $500M in budget cuts, threatening the very infrastructure of the Mass Save program.

Program Administrators (PAs) are increasingly pushing cost containment measures directly onto the local contractors who perform the work. This unsustainable model risks bankrupting the specialized workforce needed to meet the Commonwealth's ambitious climate goals.

Current Legislative Threats

  • arrow_right_alt

    Budgetary Compression

    Drastic reductions in program funding leading to reduced project volume and payment delays.

  • arrow_right_alt

    Unilateral Rate Freezes

    Contractors are being asked to absorb inflationary costs without commensurate pricing adjustments.

  • arrow_right_alt

Our Core Asks

MCIG is advocating for four fundamental pillars to ensure the longevity of the Massachusetts residential contracting industry.

payments

Fair Pricing

Implementation of price adjustments that reflect the real cost of labor, materials, and insurance in the current economy.

gavel

Competitive Bidding

A transparent, level playing field for all qualified contractors rather than preferential treatment for national firms.

diversity_3

Protect Income-Eligible

Maintaining specialized paths for low-income residents without degrading service quality or contractor compensation.

Preserve Autonomy

Ensuring contractors maintain control over their operations and direct relationships with their customers.
